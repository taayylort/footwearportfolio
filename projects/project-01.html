Silent-Sole Healthcare Footwear

A non-squeak sneaker engineered for Japanese hospital night shifts — slip-resistant without the noise.

Tank 01 / 13
In Progress
Hi-res hero image coming soon
The Brief

Requested by our Japan distributor for healthcare workers who needed a shoe that stayed silent on hospital floors during night shifts. Requirements: a wipeable durabuck/synthetic upper with mesh side panels for breathability, bungee laces for easy on/off, a pure white colorway, a wide/relaxed fit, an anti-slip outsole that stays quiet at night, and slip-in construction targeting a 10,900 yen retail price.

The Insight

Every shoe in our soft-toe line already uses a slip-resistant rubber outsole — but squeaking isn't caused by the rubber compound itself, it's caused by rubber meeting a sleek, sealed floor. That reframed the brief: this wasn't just a slip-resistant shoe, it needed to be engineered specifically to stay silent.

I also saw a bigger opportunity. Reference research into Japanese healthcare footwear — studying options from Asics and New Balance — confirmed the silhouette, quiet outsole, and price point could work well beyond Japan, so I designed with the U.S. domestic market in mind too.

Troubleshooting the Direction

Working with our technician, I mapped the open questions before committing to a direction: outsole vs. midsole compound strategy, a standard tread paired with a slip-resistant compound vs. our patented slip-resistant tread, and how to land at 10,900 yen without compromising the brief.

A premium compound + patented tread combo (Huntsman Resagrip) priced out too high. I explored three upper strategies instead, each with a tradeoff: a 90% synthetic upper (cheapest, but mesh side panels made hitting a true 90% ratio tricky), a textile-majority upper (raised questions on whether it could still meet "easy wipe clean"), and a synthetic leather upper (best durability, but risked pricing above target and outside the brief's synthetic spec).

Sketching Wide, Then Narrowing

Rather than sketch only "safe" options, I drew every direction I could think of — including a few that didn't fully match spec yet — to build a wider pool of concepts to pull from. I kept the priority on a clean, sleek profile with only necessary details, then built CADs of my strongest concepts on the correct last.

Status

This project is still in progress — more sketches, CADs, and the final selected direction to come.
